BaoCode on your phone and your privacy

Updated 21 October 2026

BaoCode on your phone connects to BaoCode on your computers, the desktop app, so you can follow its agents, answer them and send them messages while you’re away. This page says what the phone app keeps, what it sends, and to whom.

Between your phone and your computer, and nowhere else

The app talks only to the computers you pair it with: directly over your local network, or over the internet to an address you set up (your computer’s, or your own Cloudflare Tunnel). Everything between the two is end-to-end encrypted (X25519 and XSalsa20-Poly1305), with keys made on your devices when you pair them; you compare a security code on both screens as you do. There is no BaoCode server in between, and we have no way to see what you send.

What stays on your phone

The app keeps its own key and the list of computers paired with it (their names, addresses and keys), in its own storage, which only it can read. Conversations are shown as your computer sends them and aren’t saved on the phone. Uninstalling the app removes all of it.

What it asks permission for

  • Camera: to scan the QR code your computer shows to pair, and to take a photo you send to an agent.
  • Photos: to send the photos you pick to an agent. It sees only the ones you pick.
  • Local network: to find your computers on the same network, and connect to them.
  • Notifications: to tell you when an agent asks you something or finishes. They are shown by the phone itself; no push service is involved.

Your computer and your AI provider

What you send from the phone, messages, answers and photos, goes to BaoCode on your computer, as if you had typed it there. The agents there send their conversations to the AI model you use (Anthropic’s for Claude Code, or a provider you set up yourself), under that provider’s terms.

What it never does

  • No analytics, no tracking, no ads. The app doesn’t send anything about you or how you use it to anyone.
  • No account: there is nothing to sign up for.
  • No selling of data, and no handing it to third parties.

Apple may share crash reports with us if you agreed to share them with app developers (on your iPhone, Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements); TestFlight shares the feedback you choose to send. Neither includes your conversations.

Questions?

The encrypted link is open source, in BaoCode’s code. For anything else, open an issue.
